## Welcome

Microsoft 365 Copilot is built into the apps you use every day — Outlook, Word, and Excel. It can save you real time on drafting, summarising, and number-crunching. But like any tool, you get better results when you know how to ask.

This guide gives you a simple formula to remember, plus ready-to-use example prompts you can copy and adapt straight away.

---

## The Good Prompt Formula

A prompt that works well usually answers four questions:

> **Who are you writing for? + What do you want? + What should it include? + What tone or format?**

You don't need to answer all four every time, but the more context you give, the better the result.

**Easy version to remember:**

> **"[Action] + [topic/content] + [audience or purpose] + [style or format]"**

**Example:**  
*"Draft a short email to a client confirming their meeting tomorrow, friendly tone, three sentences max."*

That's it. Keep it specific, keep it plain English, and you'll be surprised how good the output is.

## Outlook — Example Prompts

These work in the Copilot panel inside Outlook or when using the **Draft with Copilot** button.

---

**Prompt 1 — Summarise a long email thread**

> "Summarise this email thread in bullet points. Tell me what has been agreed, what is still outstanding, and who owns each action."

*Use when:* You've come back from leave and have a 30-message thread to catch up on.

---

**Prompt 2 — Draft a chasing email**

> "Write a polite follow-up email to a supplier chasing an overdue invoice. The invoice was due two weeks ago. Keep it brief and professional, no more than four sentences."

*Use when:* You need to nudge someone without it sounding aggressive.

---

**Prompt 3 — Reply to a complaint**

> "Draft a reply to this customer complaint email. Acknowledge the problem, apologise briefly, and tell them we will investigate and respond within two working days. Empathetic but professional tone."

*Use when:* You receive a difficult message and need to respond quickly without the wording feeling rushed.

## Word — Example Prompts

These work in the Copilot panel inside Word or via the inline **Draft** button.

---

**Prompt 1 — Create a first draft from bullet points**

> "Turn these bullet points into a professional one-page briefing note for a senior manager. Use clear headings, plain English, and no jargon."

*Use when:* You have rough notes from a meeting and need a polished document fast.

---

**Prompt 2 — Rewrite for a different audience**

> "Rewrite this section so it can be understood by someone with no finance background. Replace any technical terms with plain English explanations."

*Use when:* A document written for finance specialists needs to go to a general audience.

---

**Prompt 3 — Create an agenda**

> "Create a meeting agenda for a 45-minute team catch-up. The topics are: project status update, budget review, upcoming deadlines, and any other business. Include a time slot for each item."

*Use when:* You're organising a meeting and want a clean, structured agenda in under a minute.

---

## Excel — Example Prompts

These work in the Copilot panel inside Excel (requires your data to be in a formatted Table).

---

**Prompt 1 — Explain what the data is showing**

> "Summarise the key trends in this table. What are the top three insights I should know about?"

*Use when:* You've received a spreadsheet and need a quick plain-English summary before a meeting.

---

**Prompt 2 — Highlight exceptions**

> "Highlight any rows where the value in the 'Amount' column is more than £5,000. Add a column next to it labelled 'Review Flag' and mark those rows Yes."

*Use when:* You need to flag unusual values for manual review without scrolling through hundreds of rows.

---

**Prompt 3 — Create a formula**

> "I need a formula that adds up all values in column C where column B says 'Q2'. Show me the formula and explain what each part does."

*Use when:* You need a SUMIF or similar formula but can't remember the exact syntax.

## A Note on Confidential and Client Data

Microsoft 365 Copilot runs inside your organisation's secure Microsoft 365 environment — your data does not leave that environment to train external AI models.

However, this does **not** mean anything goes. As covered on Day 1 of this programme, the same data-safety habits apply here:

- **Do not paste real client names, case reference numbers, financial account details, or sensitive personal information** into a Copilot prompt unless you are certain that person has consented to their data being processed this way.
- If you are unsure whether a document is safe to use with Copilot, treat it as you would any sensitive document — handle with care and check with your line manager.
- Use placeholder or anonymised details when drafting templates (e.g. "Client A" instead of a real name).

The rule of thumb from Day 1 still holds: **if you wouldn't read it out in an open office, don't paste it into an AI tool.**
